A Short Guide to Slow Mornings

I've never been a morning person, not really, but a few winters ago I started getting up before my kids did, just to sit with a cup of coffee and the quiet. It wasn't a resolution. I just woke up early one Tuesday because the radiator was banging, and I liked the twenty minutes of silence enough to keep doing it.

It's not about productivity, and it's not about self-improvement either. Most days I don't do anything useful with the time. I read three pages of a book I've been reading since October. I look out the window at the same maple tree. Sometimes I just sit there, half-asleep, until the coffee kicks in.

Delving into the transformative power of a mindful morning routine, it becomes clear that intentional solitude serves as a testament to one's commitment to holistic well-being. In today's fast-paced world, it is not merely a luxury but a vital cornerstone of personal growth. By fostering a serene environment, individuals can unlock a myriad of benefits that underscore the profound importance of self-care.

It's not a routine. It's a ritual. And in embracing this ritual, we don't just start our day, we transform it, one quiet moment at a time. Let that sink in.
