## Human samples (`eval/samples/human/`)

Each file was fetched verbatim from a public source recorded in
[`human/SOURCES.tsv`](human/SOURCES.tsv). The licence depends on the source:

- **Wikipedia** text is CC BY-SA. Attribute it to Wikipedia and the article title, with a link
  to the article, per the source URL and revision recorded in `SOURCES.tsv`.
- **Stack Exchange** text is CC BY-SA, per the Stack Exchange terms of service. Note that
  Stack Exchange content posted before June 2018 is licensed CC BY-SA 3.0, not 4.0, and reusing
  3.0-licensed content under 4.0 terms is not automatic. So: CC BY-SA (3.0 or 4.0, as recorded
  per file in `SOURCES.tsv`). Attribute it to the answer's author and a link to the answer, per
  the source URL in `SOURCES.tsv`.
- **Project Gutenberg** excerpts are public domain (pre-1929 publication).
- **US National Park Service** (and other US federal government) pages are public domain as
  US Government Works.

If you reuse a file from this set, check its row in `SOURCES.tsv` for the exact source, date and
licence before you attribute or redistribute it.
